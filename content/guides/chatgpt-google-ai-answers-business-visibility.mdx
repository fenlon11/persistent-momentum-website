Customers now ask AI assistants about the kind of thing you sell, and those assistants don't always describe your business correctly. In November 2026 OpenAI opened a beta that lets businesses claim a listing in ChatGPT and see how people engage with them there. In the same month Google moved Search onto its Gemini 4 Hydrogen model for all users worldwide. This guide shows you how to check what both say about you, and what you can actually change.

## What changed

**ChatGPT.** OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Through a tool called Business Hub, you can claim your business, manage its information and see insights on how people engage with it in ChatGPT. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week.

**Google Search.** Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, wrote that Gemini 4 Hydrogen is now available globally in Search for all users, and Google calls it the biggest model upgrade to Search yet. Google says Search now handles multi-part questions, typos and very specific requests. It also says Search links to a broader range of sites and shows more images, comparison tables and custom tools in its answers.

## Step 1: Ask the assistants what they say about you

Write down 5 to 10 questions a real customer would ask before buying from you. Include your business name, your product or service category, and at least one longer question with several parts, since Google says Search now handles those better.

Run each question in ChatGPT and in Google Search. For each answer, note:

- Whether your business is mentioned at all
- Whether the details are right: what you sell, your prices, who you serve
- Which websites the answer links to, and whether yours is one of them

Save the results in a dated document so you have something to compare against later.

## Step 2: Claim your business in ChatGPT

OpenAI says any eligible business located where ChatGPT is available can claim its business through [Business Hub](https://openai.com/business/businesses-in-chatgpt?utm_source=openai&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=business_agents_launch&utm_content=smb_launch_primary_cta). After you're verified, you can update key details and add product feeds. A product feed is a list of your products with their pricing and availability.

There are a few shortcuts:

- **Shopify merchants** can connect their store from Business Hub or from the Shopify admin to speed up verification. OpenAI says eligible Shopify products are already in ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog.
- **Existing OpenAI Enterprise customers and advertisers** can work with their OpenAI representative to claim their business.

## Step 3: Keep your details current

OpenAI frames Business Hub as a way to give ChatGPT accurate, up-to-date information. It isn't a way to write ChatGPT's answers for it. That makes your feed and details the part you control. Choose one person to update them whenever prices, stock or services change, and add it to the same checklist you use when you update your website.

## Step 4: Read the insights, and know their limits

Business Hub shows how often your business is mentioned in ChatGPT and how many website clicks you get from it. It also shows discovery topics, which are the common themes in conversations where your website is cited. OpenAI says these can help you see how you're represented and where to improve the information you share.

There are limits. The insights are aggregated, and they only appear once there are enough conversations to meet certain thresholds. You won't see what customers typed. OpenAI says: "We don't share users' conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses." If you run a small business, you may see very little data at first. Keep doing your manual checks from Step 1 in the meantime.

## Step 5: Give Google's AI answers good pages to link to

Google's post doesn't announce a tool that lets business owners manage how they appear in these answers. What it describes is how Search now answers questions. It uses query fan-out, which means it splits your question into subtopics and runs searches on them at the same time, then links to a wider range of sites, including ones people may not have found before.

Google doesn't say what it looks for in a site, so treat this as a reasonable bet, not a rule. Our read is that your website is the lever you control. Look at the multi-part questions from Step 1, and make sure a page on your site answers each part plainly: who it's for, what it costs and how it compares with the alternatives. Then run the same questions again in a few weeks and see whether the links change.

## Step 6: Decide whether you need a Business Agent or ads

Once you've claimed your business, you may be able to build, test and launch a Business Agent. It's a chat that answers customers' questions about what you offer. For now, Business Agents are only available to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States. OpenAI's [Business Agents policy](https://openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/) says it considers both the business category and the agent's intended use before allowing publication, and that business verification alone does not guarantee agent eligibility. OpenAI says these conversations are clearly labeled and kept separate from ChatGPT's own answers.

You can also sponsor your Business Agent and use it as the landing point for ChatGPT Ads. OpenAI says sponsored placements are clearly labeled and separate from ChatGPT's answers. Get your listing accurate before you pay to send people to it.

## What it means for your business

These tools don't change one basic fact: you can't edit what an AI assistant says about you. What you can do is supply accurate information, make sure the pages it links to answer real questions, and check again regularly. Treat it like reviewing your search listings: a short monthly job with one owner, not a one-time project.

Here's an example. Say you run an online store on Shopify selling handmade candles. In your Step 1 check, ChatGPT recommends you but quotes last season's price, and Google's answer to "unscented soy candle that ships to Canada and costs under 30 dollars" links to two competitors and not to you. You'd connect your Shopify store in Business Hub and confirm the feed shows current prices. Then you'd add a plain page to your site covering your unscented range, prices and shipping. A month later, you'd rerun the same questions and compare them with your saved notes.

There are three things to watch. Businesses in ChatGPT is still a beta, so features may change. OpenAI says it plans to expand Businesses in ChatGPT and Business Agents to more markets and business categories, so if you don't qualify for an agent today, check again later. And if Business Hub shows mentions but few clicks, read your discovery topics: they show which questions bring you up and where your information is thin.
