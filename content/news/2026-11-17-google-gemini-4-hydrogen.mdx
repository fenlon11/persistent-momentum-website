On November 12, Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a model built for fast, everyday coding and agent work. It costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. It now runs in Google Search for all users worldwide, and also in the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Gemini for business and the Gemini app, so it changes both what your customers see when they search and what you pay to build on Gemini.

## What Google released

Google positions Hydrogen as the fast worker in the Gemini 4 family. Its frontier models, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon, handle deep, long reasoning. Hydrogen is meant for the many smaller steps in a workflow, like writing code, running tests, checking changes and pulling documents together. It can work alone or alongside Carbon and Argon.

The key numbers all come from [Google's announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/) by Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP at Google DeepMind:

- **Pricing:** $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. Tokens are the small chunks of text a model reads and writes, and they are what you get billed for. The price stays the same across the full 1 million-token context window (how much text the model can read at once).
- **Thinking levels:** four settings: low, medium, high (the default) and a new xhigh. You pay only for as much reasoning as a task needs.
- **Benchmarks, per Google:** Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, against 50 for Argon, and 54 at xhigh.
- **Compared with Gemini 3.8 Flash, per Google:** Hydrogen finishes tasks 2.5 times faster at the default setting. Its Terminal-Bench 4.1 score goes from 18.2% to 57%, and it makes more than 5x fewer fabricated claims. Google says early partner tests found Hydrogen ran 2x to 2.8x faster and used 12% to 23% fewer tokens than 3.8 Flash.

Google also added Orchestrator to Google Antigravity. Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen within a single session. Google says that on Terminal-Bench 4.0 it came close to Carbon's success rate while using 24% less quota on average and finishing 19% faster.

## Where you can use it

Google says Hydrogen began rolling out globally on November 12:

- **Developers:** the Gemini API in Google AI Studio and Android Studio, plus Google Antigravity for paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. Setup details are in Google's [developer post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-4-hydrogen/).
- **Businesses:** Gemini for business through the API or the model drop-down, Google Antigravity, and Gemini in Google Sheets for eligible business subscriptions.
- **Everyone:** Google Search and the model drop-down in the Gemini app. Free Gemini app users get Hydrogen through auto model selection when it's the right model for the prompt.

## What changes in Search

Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, wrote: "This is the biggest model upgrade to Search yet." The [Search post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/) says Search now handles multi-part questions, typos and very specific requests better. Google also upgraded query fan-out, where Search runs several queries at once across the subtopics of your question. Google says this links people to a wider range of sites, including ones they might not have found before. Answers will also show more images, structured responses, comparison tables and custom tools, in all supported AI Mode languages.

## What it means for your business

There are two things worth doing this month.

First, if you already pay for Gemini 3.8 Flash in a workflow, test the two models side by side. Say you use it to sort support tickets or summarize sales calls. Run a week's worth of the same inputs through Hydrogen at the default high setting and again at low. Then compare accuracy, speed and total cost per task. Output tokens cost five times as much as input tokens, so long answers drive the bill. At list price, a job that reads 2 million tokens and writes 500,000 costs $3 for input and $3.75 for output. The gains listed above are Google's own benchmark results. Your own test is the one that counts.

Second, check how your business shows up in Search. Google says answers will lean more on comparison tables and structured responses, and that fan-out will bring up a wider set of sites. If people compare your products or services with others, make sure your pages state prices, specs and differences plainly. Pick a few multi-part questions your customers actually ask, such as "best X under $Y that does Z", and run them yourself to see which sites the answers link to. Then watch your search traffic over the next few weeks for changes.
